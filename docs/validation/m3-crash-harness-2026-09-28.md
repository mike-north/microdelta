## Observation and limits of diagnosis

On documentation-only PR [#69](https://github.com/mike-north/microdelta/pull/69), push Check [36457852422](https://github.com/mike-north/microdelta/actions/runs/36457852422) failed its Node 24 lost-acknowledgment case: `killedAt` expected `SIGKILL`, but got `null`. The parent received an ordinary worker result, and the old harness omitted the worker's error from the assertion. A separate PR Check [36457859077](https://github.com/mike-north/microdelta/actions/runs/36457859077) passed on Node 20, 22 and 24 at the same `e14bb724` head. The failed CI worker error was not captured, so its cause remains unknown.

Temporary probes against runtime `aaa9bcd`, retained in the separate diagnostics checkout at `.test-build/diag-pr69/`, established a possible mechanism: six of six 1–5 ms leases ended at status 3, with no signal or fault line and a `StaleWriterError` reporting expiry. Other author, observer and wrong-intent errors could also produce status 3 without a fault line. At 300 ms, 20/20 idle, 20/20 under 18 busy loops, and 20/20 under that load with scheduling priority reduced reached the planned `SIGKILL`; eight complete crash suites under load passed. Loaded 1, 2 and 3 second leases reached 20/20 kills each. The adjacent 250 ms workspace recovery fixture passed ten loaded runs; this issue did not change it. Acquisition to the selected publication boundary measured about 8–11 ms idle and 14–23 ms loaded in those probes. Those observations support giving the fixture finite headroom and reporting missed kills; they neither reproduce nor explain the historical CI failure conclusively.

## Test-first repair and resulting behavior

The planned-fault tests in `packages/core/test/acceptance/planned-fault.test.ts` were committed first (`2836633`) with a stub that accepted any worker exit, matching the old harness's missing guard. The first run, `.test-build/issue70/logs/04-RED-planned-fault.log`, failed 9 of 12 assertions. Two of those failures were real independent-process author and observer cases: the old behavior returned normally when the planned kill was unreachable, so the expected error was absent. Six failures came from pure judgement cases against the deliberate stub; the remaining failure exposed the old fault trace's missing structured boundary fields. These categories are distinct. The guard implementation (`7558a5d`) then passed the planned-fault and crash-recovery suites, 21/21 (`05-GREEN-guard.log`).

For a planned fault, `scenario().run()` now accepts only a `SIGKILL` with a single final fault trace whose role, occurrence and before/after boundary match the request. A miss throws `MissedPlannedKillError` with requested fault, status and signal, actual fault trace or its absence, parsed worker error, and stderr. A genuine worker error therefore remains visible. Runs without a planned fault preserve their ordinary status-3 result semantics. The relevant assertions include wrong trace, trace without kill, kill without trace, and real worker author and observer failures.

The seven existing real SQLite publication-kill boundaries remain in `crash-recovery.test.ts`. Killed and relevant follow-up requests now have a 2,000 ms writer lease. Before a later normal request, the test reads History's stored holder and `expiresAt`, then waits until the host time passes that expiry by 50 ms. The wait has a 2,050 ms monotonic budget, diagnoses a missing or changed holder, an unexpectedly distant expiry, and an exhausted budget, and does not take or mutate the writer. The first headroom run passed 9/9 real crash cases (`07-GREEN-headroom.log`); their per-test durations reached at most 2,584 ms (`08-crash-durations.txt`). A deterministic simulated backward-clock test first failed 1 of 15 (`09-RED-bounded-wait.log`): the earlier wall-clock-only loop kept waiting until the test's escape guard at 205,550 simulated ms instead of failing within the 2,050 ms budget. After the monotonic bound (`592073a`), the focused lease, fault and crash suites passed 27/27 (`10-GREEN-bounded-wait.log`). The code comments carry the stable purpose and boundary; these measurements belong here.
